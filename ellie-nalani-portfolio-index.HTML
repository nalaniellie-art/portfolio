Case Study 01 · Retention

Rescuing a high-value client who had quietly stopped engaging

A client managing multiple bookings through the platform had gone silent for three weeks — no responses, no new orders. In most CS setups, this gets flagged too late or not at all.

I noticed the drop in activity during a routine check of engagement data in Mixpanel. Instead of waiting for a cancellation notice, I reached out proactively — acknowledged the silence, asked the right questions, and uncovered that they'd had a poor service experience that hadn't been formally raised.

I owned the complaint end-to-end: investigated what went wrong, communicated clearly about the fix, followed up twice over the next two weeks, and got them back to full engagement within 30 days. No discount offered. Just accountability and consistency.

Case Study 02 · Escalation

Managing a wave of simultaneous complaints during a service outage

On one of the busiest days at MM Studio, a payment processing issue caused multiple client transactions to fail at the till. Within 20 minutes, I had 15+ clients raising issues simultaneously — in-person and via WhatsApp — while new walk-ins continued to arrive.

I made three fast decisions: communicate the issue transparently rather than stall, triage by urgency (clients who'd already paid vs those mid-transaction), and create a manual log to ensure no one was missed once systems recovered.

I handled every interaction without calling for management backup. Resolved all cases by end of day. Not one client left without a confirmed outcome.

Case Study 03 · Operations

Clearing a 3-day ticket backlog inherited from an unresponsive queue

While managing remote support freelance work, I onboarded a new client mid-project who had a Zendesk queue that hadn't been touched in 72 hours. Over 40 open tickets, ranging from simple account questions to unresolved order disputes and one potentially churning enterprise-level user.

I triaged by impact: revenue risk first, then time-sensitivity, then routine. I built a rapid-response template system for the repeating issues (which were 60% of the queue) and gave every high-risk ticket a personal response within 2 hours of starting.

Queue cleared in 11 hours across two days. The at-risk enterprise user was retained. Three recurring issue types were flagged to the product team as systemic problems.

Ticket triage by impact Template design Risk identification Async communication Feedback routing Speed + quality balance

Case Study 04 · Commercial

Resolving a pricing dispute that was two days from becoming a lost client

A client at MM Studio had been charged incorrectly for a service package — a billing error on our side. They came in frustrated, had already told two other clients about the issue, and had decided they weren't coming back.

I didn't deflect or make promises I couldn't keep. I pulled the transaction history on the spot, confirmed the error immediately, and owned the mistake without hedging. I offered a direct resolution — a credit on their account plus a complimentary follow-up — and processed it before they left.

They returned the following week. More importantly, they mentioned the experience positively to the same clients they'd previously complained to.

Case Study 05 · Analytics

Using product analytics to catch a declining account before it raised a ticket

While managing a remote client's support operations, I set up a habit of reviewing Mixpanel engagement data weekly — not just responding to incoming issues. I noticed one account's session frequency had dropped by 60% over three weeks, despite no support tickets being raised.

I reached out with a check-in framed around their specific use case — not a generic "how are things?" message. They revealed they'd encountered a configuration problem they hadn't bothered to report, assuming it would require too much back-and-forth to fix.

I walked them through the fix over Slack in one conversation. They were back to full usage within 24 hours. No ticket. No escalation. Just proactive attention to data.
